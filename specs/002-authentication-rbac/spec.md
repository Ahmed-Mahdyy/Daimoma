## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-25

- Q: For the first release, how should accounts be created and who should be able to see an employee’s draft requests and timesheets? → A: Option B — one organization per deployment; HR provisions accounts (no self-signup); each employee alone sees their drafts. Managers see non-draft work of their current direct reports; HR sees non-draft organization work. Managers and HR retain access to their own employee workspace (D-003 approved by owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: If an employee has no eligible manager to review submitted work—for example, the manager is inactive or would be reviewing their own work—what should happen? → A: HR is the responsible reviewer in that case; route to an eligible HR reviewer, never the submitter. If no eligible HR exists, block submission rather than leave unowned work (D-004 fallback decision, owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: When an eligible manager does approve a request, should HR also review it before it becomes final? → A: Option A — an additional HR stage is required only for request types explicitly configured to need it; otherwise manager approval is final. HR-as-manager fallback remains separate from this second stage (D-004 request-type routing decision, owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: If a request type requires HR review, but HR is already reviewing it instead of an unavailable manager, should that HR decision complete the review or should a different HR person review it again? → A: Option A — the eligible non-self HR fallback's single decision completes the request; no duplicate HR stage follows (D-004 fallback-stage decision, owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: If HR changes an employee’s manager while a request or timesheet is still waiting for the old manager’s review, who should own that pending review? → A: Option A — atomically reassign pending steps to the new eligible manager, or to eligible non-self HR if the new manager cannot review; revoke the former manager's access immediately and preserve completed decisions. A manager change that cannot leave pending work assigned to an eligible reviewer must be blocked with an actionable explanation (D-004 reassignment decision, owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: What employee data may be used while we build and test Phase 002, including any early staging site? → A: Option A — synthetic data only in development, tests and early staging; no real or de-identified employee records until separate retention, MFA, privacy and deployment approvals and verification (D-008 synthetic-only portion approved by owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: How long should a sign-in session, invitation link, and password-recovery link remain valid? → A: Owner specified 2 hours idle sign-in timeout, 48-hour invitation link, 30-minute password-recovery link, and confirmed a 24-hour absolute session limit in a follow-up (latest answer "24 is ok"; D-009 lifetimes approved 2026-09-25). Invitation/recovery tokens remain single-use; expiry or logout requires explicit reauthentication, never automatic resubmission.
- Q: How should the app slow down repeated failed sign-in attempts? → A: Option A with a 5-minute counting window: after 5 failures per account or 20 per source IP, throttle further sign-ins for 5 minutes; no permanent account lock (D-009 sign-in limits approved by owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: How should we limit repeated password-recovery requests and failed invitation-link attempts? → A: Recommended option A — recovery requests limited to 3 per email per hour and 20 per source IP per 5 minutes; failed invitation-link attempts limited to 20 per source IP per 5 minutes. Recovery responses remain generic whether the account exists or a limit is reached (D-009 public-entry limits approved by owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: What environment should we plan for an early Phase 002 staging site that uses synthetic data only? → A: Initially option A, revised by owner 2026-09-25: self-hosted target rather than managed hosting; local development should use no new paid service. Browser JavaScript email delivery was floated, not approved: invitation/recovery tokens must be generated and delivered server-side without exposing delivery credentials. No provider, SMTP service, region, budget or deployment is approved (D-007 partial revision).
- Q: Should Phase 002 stay on the development machine until a self-hosted staging server is approved, or is a server already available for synthetic-data staging? → A: Option A — Phase 002 is local-development only; self-hosted synthetic staging is deferred until a server is available and deployment is explicitly approved. Phase 002 staging verification becomes a local production-like smoke check, not a live deployment (D-007 staging timing approved by owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: Which second sign-in factor should HR administrators use when privileged MFA is introduced? → A: Defer choosing and implementing privileged MFA until a later stage; Phase 002 stays synthetic-only. No real employee data may be used until a separate MFA method is approved, implemented and tested along with privacy and retention gates (D-008 deferral approved by owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: For local development, how should Django deliver invitation and password-recovery emails so links can be tested? → A: Option A — Django's built-in console/file email backend; no extra service, no credentials, full test emails visible in the terminal or a local folder. Any real SMTP/mail service remains a later deployment approval (D-007 local mail sink approved by owner 2026-09-25).
- Q: Which profile fields may an employee edit themselves, and which stay HR-controlled? → A: Option A — employees may edit their own display name, phone and language preference; HR alone controls email, legal name, department, role, manager and account status. Attempts to modify HR-controlled fields through the employee profile endpoint are rejected (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Who can see the employee directory, and how much of it? → A: Option B — no general staff directory: employees see only their own profile; managers see only their current direct reports; HR sees the whole organization. Directory-related endpoints must scope accordingly and return a non-enumerating 404 for invisible profiles (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: What do employees type to sign in? → A: Option A — email address only; no separate username. Sign-in compares against the normalized account email (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Besides invitation/recovery emails, should the app also email people about events such as approvals? → A: Option A — in-app notifications only in Phase 002; notification emails beyond onboarding/recovery are deferred and require an approved mail transport first (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: What makes an acceptable password when an employee sets one via invitation or recovery? → A: Option A — minimum 12 characters including at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, and one digit (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Can an employee be signed in on multiple devices or browsers at the same time? → A: Option A — multiple concurrent sessions are allowed; each session is still bound by the 2-hour idle / 24-hour absolute limits, and deactivation or a completed password recovery invalidates all existing sessions (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: If an invited employee does not use their link within 48 hours, what should HR be able to do? → A: Option A — HR can re-issue a fresh invitation: a new single-use token with a new 48-hour window; the previous link is permanently invalidated. No new account is needed (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: On a user's first visit, before they have picked a language, what should the UI show? → A: Option A — always default to English; once the user chooses a language, the saved preference takes over on every subsequent visit (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Should a signed-in user be able to see and manage their own active sessions? → A: Option B — no session-list UI in Phase 002; logout ends only the current session. Session invalidation still happens silently server-side on deactivation or completed password recovery (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Who can view the audit history of significant actions? → A: Option C — HR sees all audit events for the organization; managers may see events affecting their current direct reports; employees see no audit log and learn of relevant events through notifications (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: If HR deactivates an employee account, can it be reactivated later? → A: Option A — HR can reactivate: the account, history and reporting line are restored; all prior sessions remain dead and the employee signs in fresh. Deactivation is never a delete (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Can a signed-in employee change their own password from settings? → A: Option B — yes: a settings form requires the current password and the same 12-character policy; on success all other sessions are invalidated, so a suspected intruder is locked out (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Who manages the department list? → A: Option A — HR adds, renames and deactivates departments from settings; deactivation preserves history for previously assigned employees, departments are never deleted (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: Must every employee belong to a department, and what happens when HR deactivates a department that still has members? → A: Option A — department membership is optional; deactivating a department with members is allowed and leaves those members without a department (owner-approved 2026-09-25).
- Q: How is the local development environment provisioned? → A: Fully dockerized — backend runs in a `python:3.12` container (owner-specified), frontend in a Node LTS container, PostgreSQL in its own container; nothing needs installing on the host beyond Docker (owner-approved 2026-09-25).

## User Scenarios & Testing

### User Story 1 - Secure personal access (Priority: P1)

As an employee I can accept an invitation, sign in/out, recover access, and maintain permitted profile fields.

**Independent Test**: Complete onboarding/login/profile/logout and invalid/expired/inactive-account cases without requests or timesheets being implemented.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:
1. Given an active invited account, when accepting a valid unused invitation and signing in, then personal navigation and permitted profile details appear.
2. Given invalid credentials or an inactive account, when signing in, then access is denied without disclosing unnecessary account information.
3. Given a recovery token older than 30 minutes or an invitation older than 48 hours, or either token already used, when attempting acceptance, then it fails safely; a valid recovery invalidates access according to the approved session policy.
4. Given logout, 2 hours of inactivity, or 24 hours after sign-in even with activity, when attempting another protected operation with the old session, then access fails, stale user data is cleared from the client, and unsent work is not silently resubmitted.

### User Story 2 - HR manages employees and reporting lines (Priority: P1)

As HR I can provision/deactivate employees and maintain eligible roles, departments, and reporting lines without damaging history.

**Independent Test**: Build a synthetic two-team directory, change one reporting line, and verify protected relationships and access immediately.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:
1. Given HR scope in the single-organization deployment, when creating an employee, then an account/profile are created consistently and onboarding delivery is observable/retryable; self-signup is unavailable.
2. Given an employee or manager, when attempting role/manager/account-status changes, then unauthorized changes fail without modifying data.
3. Given a self-manager, reporting cycle, or foreign-organization relationship, when saving, then the invalid change is rejected.
4. Given an account with historical work, when deactivating, then history remains and the account loses access. Given a manager change with pending requests or timesheets, reassign those steps atomically to the new eligible manager or eligible non-self HR; revoke former-manager access while preserving completed decisions. Reject the change if no eligible reviewer remains.
5. Given the last eligible HR administrator, when trying to remove its privileges, then the operation is blocked under the approved policy.

### User Story 3 - Predictable scoped workspace (Priority: P2)

As any role I see only permitted navigation and information, with consistent session, error, and notification behavior.

**Independent Test**: Compare Employee/Manager/HR sessions across two teams and a second-organization negative fixture, including direct resource access.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:
1. Given a manager, when requesting another team's records, any employee's drafts (except their own), or out-of-scope directory choices, then no out-of-scope detail or count is disclosed. HR likewise cannot view another employee's drafts; HR may view non-draft organization work.
2. Given a role/reporting-line change, when making the next request, then current eligibility applies rather than stale login-time permissions. If the assigned manager is ineligible at submission, an eligible non-self HR reviewer becomes responsible; submission is blocked if none exists.
3. Given failed or slow requests, when using the shell/profile/directory, then safe error/loading/empty/validation/disabled/success behavior is provided.
4. Given a security-relevant administrative action, when inspecting authorized history, then actor, target, action, and time are available without secrets.

### Edge Cases

Email casing/duplicates, concurrent role or manager changes, failed invitation delivery, expired/used links, inactive fallback reviewer, cross-organization references, missing profile, expired session during editing, malicious protected fields, invalid CSRF, brute-force attempts, and manager/HR users' own employee capability.

## Requirements

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: Provide secure invitation/sign-in/sign-out/recovery and account deactivation behavior; reject invalid/expired/replayed access. Sign-in uses the normalized account email as the identifier — no username. End sessions after 2 hours idle or 24 hours absolute from sign-in, whichever comes first; single-use invitations expire after 48 hours and single-use password-recovery links after 30 minutes. For sign-in failures, count attempts per account and per source IP in a 5-minute window; after 5/account or 20/IP failures throttle further sign-ins for 5 minutes, without permanent account lock. Limit recovery requests to 3 per email per hour and 20 per source IP per 5 minutes, and failed invitation-link attempts to 20 per source IP per 5 minutes; recovery acknowledgements stay generic regardless of account existence or throttling. Multiple concurrent sessions are permitted, each bound by the same limits. Passwords set via invitation or recovery require minimum 12 characters with at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, and one digit.
- **FR-002**: Enforce Employee/Manager/HR roles and organization/ownership/direct-team boundaries on every information and mutation surface in a single-organization deployment. Draft requests and timesheets are visible only to their owner; managers may view non-drafts of current direct reports and HR may view non-drafts across the organization. Manager and HR retain their own Employee capability. No self-approval or implicit superuser rights.
- **FR-003**: Allow users to view and edit only approved ordinary profile fields — employees may change their own display name, phone and language preference; email, legal name, department, role, manager and account status are HR-controlled and must be rejected on the employee endpoint.
- **FR-004**: Allow HR to provision, deactivate, and reactivate employees, assign departments/managers/business roles, re-issue expired invitations, and manage approved organization settings — including adding, renaming and deactivating (never deleting) departments — without destroying history; department membership is optional and deactivating a department leaves its members unassigned; no public or employee self-signup.
- **FR-005**: Reject reporting cycles, self-manager relationships, ineligible reviewers, last-HR removal, and cross-organization references; serialize conflicting administrative changes. Where the ordinary manager is ineligible or missing at submission, assign an eligible non-self HR reviewer responsible for the item; if none exists, block submission. After an eligible manager approves a request, only types explicitly configured to require HR proceed to a second HR stage; otherwise manager approval is final. If the first-stage reviewer is an eligible HR fallback, that single HR decision is final even for an HR-required type; do not create a duplicate HR stage. On a reporting-line change, atomically reassign outstanding manager steps to the new eligible manager or eligible non-self HR fallback, revoke old-manager access, and preserve completed history; reject the administrative change if no eligible reviewer can own every pending step.
- **FR-006**: Record significant administrative/security actions in restricted non-editable history without credentials or sensitive free-text payloads. Read scope: HR sees the organization's full audit; managers see events affecting their current direct reports; employees have no audit access.
- **FR-007**: Establish recipient-only durable in-app notification records and safe target access; deduplicate events and separate transient feedback from history. Phase 002 sends no notification emails beyond onboarding/recovery; event email alerts are deferred until a mail transport is approved.
- **FR-008**: Provide the approved responsive authenticated shell, account/profile/directory/settings forms, accessible states, and safe session-expiry recovery.
- **FR-009**: Provide bounded search/filter/sort/pagination for permitted employee information — employees can retrieve only their own profile, managers only their current direct reports, HR the organization — with explicit editable fields and safe error messages; there is no organization-wide employee directory for ordinary users.
- **FR-010**: Ensure every critical account, scope, CSRF, session, abuse, and administrative boundary has automated happy-path and negative tests.
- **FR-011**: Establish reproducible application/runtime configuration, versioned schema evolution, documented interface contracts, and automated verification before domain implementation.
- **FR-012**: Build and test locally without new paid services; Phase 002 has no remote staging — a future self-hosted synthetic-only staging site is deferred until a server is available and deployment is separately approved, not a managed-provider deployment. Phase 002 ends with a local production-like smoke check. Do not claim a live staging flow until server, private PostgreSQL, server-side mail delivery, secrets and deployment are separately approved and verified. Invitation and recovery tokens must never be sent from browser JavaScript or expose mail-service credentials. Use synthetic records only in local development, tests and eventual staging; no real or de-identified employee data until separate retention, privacy, privileged MFA and operational approvals and verification.

### Key Entities

Organization, department, account, employee profile, business role, audit event, and notification. The employee-facing product does not grant database or superuser administration.

## Success Criteria

- **SC-001**: All specified role/ownership/organization negative scenarios, including manager/HR access to others' drafts and self-signup, are denied without data changes or information leakage.
- **SC-002**: Onboarding, sign-in, profile, recovery, and deactivation each have passing positive and failure scenarios.
- **SC-003**: Administrative history identifies every tested significant role/reporting-line/status change while excluding secrets.
- **SC-004**: An operator can reproduce the foundation and verify the first access flow locally under production-like settings from reviewed instructions; remote staging is deferred per D-007.
